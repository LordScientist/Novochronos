Challenge Statement

Humanity's Resting Pioneers on Alien Worlds

Over six decades of spaceflight, humanity has deployed dozens of robotic explorers into deep space. When power fades or hardware wears down, their journeys do not disappear: their physical bodies remain silently in place as irreplaceable monuments of human curiosity.

01

Data Inaccessibility

Vital coordinates, operational records, and hardware models are scattered across complex technical databases that are difficult for students and the general public to explore.

02

The Risk of Erasure

Once telemetry transmissions end, the physical presence of rovers, landers, and probes on the Moon and Mars risks fading from public awareness and collective memory.

03

Preservation Urgency

As humanity prepares for permanent lunar bases and crewed missions to Mars, preserving robotic exploration sites as protected international heritage sites is essential.

Challenge Focus: Transforming complex planetary telemetry into accessible, engaging space history.
Our Solution

Novochronos: A Living 3D Digital Museum

We developed a web-based interactive archive that turns raw space telemetry into an emotional, museum-grade journey. Accessible directly in any modern browser with zero downloads or specialized hardware.

Visual Pillar

Spatial 3D Inspection

High-fidelity 3D models with 360-degree orbital rotation, pan, zoom, and wireframe views, alongside authentic NASA mission photography.

Auditory Pillar

First-Person Stories

Spoken audio voiceovers recount each machine's breakthroughs and final operational transmissions, giving solitary hardware an authentic voice.

Tactile Pillar

Interactive Simulations

Helical momentum camera navigation and a built-in retro orbital launch simulation teach orbital physics and telemetry alignment through direct interaction.

Target Audience: Students, educators, aerospace enthusiasts, and the global public. Performance: 60 FPS across Mobile and Desktop
Visual & Technical Craft

The 3D Helical Flight Corridor

Instead of standard flat web page scrolling, Novochronos introduces a 3D spiral staircase flight path. Visitors descend in three dimensions around a towering launch stack as sentinel cards gracefully present their data.

Space Transportation System (STS) Spine

A realistic 3D model of the Space Shuttle Orbiter, External Tank, and Solid Rocket Boosters stands vertically as the central structural anchor for the helical descent.

Inertial Smooth Momentum Physics

Scroll progress drives exponential damping physics, smoothly synchronizing camera coordinates and look-at vectors without motion jitter or abrupt jumps.

Edge-to-Edge NASA Photography

3D cards feature unblocked, full-bleed authentic NASA mission photography with high-contrast technical outlines for perfect legibility over any photo.

Corridor Mathematics

Orbit Radius: 5.2 units around Shuttle axis

Flight Path: 1.35 spiral revolutions (~486 degrees)

Descent Range: Y = +3.2 (Tip) down to Y = -3.5 (Engines)

Framing: Radial outward alignment facing orbiting camera

Ensures all cards on the far side remain fully visible, creating deep 3D spatial perspective.
Stage 1 Architecture: WebGL Canvas, React Three Fiber, and Lenis Scroll Physics.
