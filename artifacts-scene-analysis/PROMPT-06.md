### 2. Scientific Objective of the Scene
To illustrate the human-scale dimensions of the 23-pound micro-rover Sojourner, demonstrate the airbag landing and petal unfolding mechanism of the Pathfinder mothership, explain the kinematics of the revolutionary 6-wheel rocker-bogie passive suspension system, visualize in-situ contact spectrometry using the Alpha Proton X-Ray Spectrometer (APXS) on volcanic rocks "Barnacle Bill" and "Yogi", recreate the catastrophic paleofloods that sculpted Ares Vallis, and celebrate Sojourner's legacy as the direct ancestor of all modern Mars rovers.

---

### 3. Verified Historical and Technical Context
- **Micromobility Prototype**: Weighing only 10.5 kg (23.1 lb) on Earth ($\approx 4.0 \text{ kg}$ on Mars), Sojourner was engineered as an ultra-low-cost technology demonstration for NASA's "Faster, Better, Cheaper" initiative.
- **Design vs. Actual Life**: Designed for a 7-sol operational baseline; operated for 83 sols, traversing 100 meters, capturing 550 rover photographs, and conducting 16 chemical analyses of rocks and soil.
- **APXS Chemical Breakthrough**: Detected high silicon dioxide ($SiO_2 \approx 55\%$) and andesitic compositions in boulder "Barnacle Bill", proving volcanic differentiation and crustal recycling.
- **Ares Vallis Paleofloods**: The landing site was chosen because ancient catastrophic water torrents debouched from Valles Marineris into Chryse Planitia, washing an immense variety of rock types from hundreds of kilometers away into one accessible depositional floodplain.
- **Generational Precursor**: Directly validated the rocker-bogie suspension and autonomy concepts scaled up for Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity, and Perseverance.

### 4. Environment Description
- **Geographic Setting**: Ares Vallis—a rock-strewn ancient catastrophic flood plain littered with semi-rounded boulders, imbricated gravel, and wind-drifted sand dunes.
- **Key Geological Targets**:
  - Boulder "Barnacle Bill": Distinctive 30 cm angular andesitic rock with rough, pitted weathering surfaces.
  - Boulder "Yogi": Large 1.5-meter rounded basalt rock resembling a crouching bear.
  - Outflow channels: Fluted bedrock ridges and scour terraces carved by prehistoric liquid water.
- **Atmospheric Environment**:
  - Daylight: Warm golden-ochre Martian sky ($T \approx -20^\circ \text{C}$ midday).
  - Night / Winter: Deep purple twilight plunging to $-100^\circ \text{C}$ with fine dry ice/frost crystals settling on the ground.

### 5. Artifact Modelling Requirements
- **Sojourner Chassis**: Compact rectangular warm electronics box (WEB) insulated with aerogel and wrapped in gold-amber Kapton foil.
- **Solar Array**: Top-mounted Gallium Arsenide solar panel ($0.22 \text{ m}^2$) with 0.75-meter whip antenna.
- **Rocker-Bogie Subsystem**: Six independently driven cleated aluminum wheels ($13 \text{ cm}$ diameter, $8 \text{ cm}$ width) mounted on stainless-steel rocker-bogie linkages.
- **Forward Optics**: Stereo monochrome hazard avoidance cameras and red laser stripe projector for autonomous proximity obstacle detection.
- **APXS Deployment Arm**: Rear-mounted deployable linkage carrying the cylindrical Alpha Proton X-Ray Spectrometer sensor head ($^{244}Cm$ source).
- **Pathfinder Lander Base**: Three unfolded triangular solar petals, deflated white Kevlar airbags bunched around the perimeter, and dual deployer ramps.

### 6. Mechanical Behaviour and Scientific Simulations
1. **Rocker-Bogie Ground Compliance**:
   - Dynamic articulation of the rocker arms and bogies as Sojourner drives over rocks up to 1.5 times the wheel diameter without tipping.
2. **APXS Alpha/X-Ray Spectrometry Simulation**:
   - Mechanical reversal of the rover, pressing the circular sensor face against the rock.
   - Transparent ray-tracing overlay: Alpha particles ($^{244}Cm$) bombarding rock atoms, producing characteristic X-ray fluorescent emissions revealing elemental composition ($Si$, $Fe$, $Al$, $Mg$).
3. **Catastrophic Outflow Paleoflood Simulation**:
   - Spectacular ghost fluid simulation: Billions of gallons of roaring, sediment-laden turbulent water rushing across Ares Vallis at 30 m/s, tumbling giant boulders into place before evaporating into the ancient sky.
