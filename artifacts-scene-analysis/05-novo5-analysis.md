## 2. Scientific & Engineering Principles

1. **Compact Folding & Mechanical Spring Deployment**:
   - Packaged inside a $1.5 \times 1.5 \text{ m}$ wedge in Quadrant 1 of the Lunar Module Descent Stage.
   - Folded chassis divided into three hinged sections (front, center, rear). Astronauts deployed it using nylon lanyards, bungees, and torsion springs, allowing it to unfold autonomously and lock into place in less than 15 minutes.
2. **Vacuum Mobility & Piano-Wire Mesh Wheel Engineering**:
   - Rubber pneumatic tires were impossible due to vacuum outgassing and extreme temperature swings ($-130^\circ \text{C}$ to $+120^\circ \text{C}$).
   - Wheels engineered from woven open-mesh zinc-coated steel piano wire ($0.8 \text{ mm}$ diameter) with titanium chevron treads riveted across the tread surface, providing flexible contact patch compliance and traction over loose regolith without puncture risk.
   - Four independent 0.25-horsepower (190 W) series-wound DC electric motors mounted inside each wheel hub with 80:1 harmonic drive transmissions.
3. **Ergonomic T-Handle Drive Control in Pressurized Suits**:
   - Traditional pedals and steering wheels were incompatible with pressurized Apollo spacesuits (A7LB).
   - Driven via a central T-handle between the seats: tilt forward for throttle/acceleration, tilt left/right for dual Ackermann four-wheel steering, pull back for dynamic disc braking.
4. **Planetary Geology Exploration & The Genesis Rock (Sample 15415)**:
   - Enabled traversing 27.8 km (17.3 miles) across Hadley-Apennine, extending the astronaut EVA exploration radius from 1 km to over 7 km from the LM.
   - Exploration of Hadley Rille (a sinuous lava tube collapse 1.5 km wide and 300 m deep) and the slopes of Mons Hadley Delta.
   - Collection of the **Genesis Rock**: anorthosite composed of 98% plagioclase feldspar crystallized $4.1 \pm 0.1 \text{ billion years}$ ago, verifying the Lunar Magma Ocean hypothesis.
5. **Remote-Controlled Ground Video Telemetry**:
   - RCA color TV camera mounted on the front console with pan, tilt, and 10:1 zoom capability, remotely operated from Houston Mission Control by Edward Fendell.
   - Parked 100 meters (300 feet) away from the Falcon descent stage to record the first live telecast of the Ascent Stage launch.
