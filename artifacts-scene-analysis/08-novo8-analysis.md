## 2. Scientific & Engineering Principles

1. **Pre-Apollo Regolith Bearing Strength & Dust Sinkage Hypothesis**:
   - In the mid-1960s, prominent astrophysicists (notably Thomas Gold) theorized that billions of years of micrometeoroid impacts had pulverized lunar regolith into a frictionless, electrostatic powder several meters deep that could swallow landing spacecraft and astronauts whole.
   - Objective of the Surveyor program: Measure in-situ soil mechanics, bearing strength, and landing dynamics to qualify the Apollo Lunar Module landing gear design.
2. **Three-Bounce Landing Dynamics**:
   - On April 20, 1967, Surveyor 3 descended into a $200 \text{ m}$ diameter crater. Highly reflective regolith caused radar altimeter anomalies, preventing the three vernier rocket engines from shutting down at the programmed 4-meter altitude.
   - The lander touched down, bounced 10 meters into the vacuum, bounced a second time (3 meters), and finally came to rest on the third touchdown when engines were manually commanded off from Earth.
3. **Surface Sampler Mechanical Trenching**:
   - Articulated pantograph trenching arm with a miniature motorized scoop ($13 \text{ cm} \times 5 \text{ cm}$).
   - Dug four distinct trenches up to 17.5 cm deep into the crater slope.
   - Determined regolith bulk density ($\approx 1.5 \text{ g/cm}^3$) and bearing strength ($\approx 5.5 \times 10^4 \text{ N/m}^2$), proving the soil exhibited cohesion and internal friction comparable to damp terrestrial sand.
4. **Apollo 12 Pinpoint Landing Rendezvous**:
   - On November 19, 1969, Pete Conrad and Alan Bean executed the first precision lunar landing in LM-6 *Intrepid*, setting down within walking distance ($183 \text{ meters} / 600 \text{ feet}$) of Surveyor 3 on the rim of Surveyor Crater.
5. **Space Weathering & Post-Recovery Metallurgical Analysis**:
   - Astronauts inspected Surveyor 3 and used shears to remove 10 kg of hardware: the vidicon TV camera, the surface sampler scoop, and tubing sections.
   - Returned to Earth in the Command Module *Yankee Clipper*: Provided the first empirical baseline data on 31 months of lunar vacuum exposure, solar UV radiation darkening, micrometeoroid pitting rates ($10^3 \text{ pits/m}^2$), and thermal cycling effects on spacecraft alloys.
