### 2. Scientific Objective of the Scene
To visualize the profound scale of outer solar system exploration, demonstrate Pioneer 10's historic traversal through the Main Asteroid Belt disproving catastrophic collision theories, simulate the intense radiation belts and turbulent atmospheric flyby of Jupiter, showcase the engineering and cryptographic meaning of the gold-anodized Pioneer Plaque designed by Carl Sagan and Frank Drake, and illustrate Pioneer 10's eternal, peaceful coasting trajectory toward the red giant star Aldebaran over a two-million-year interstellar voyage.

---

### 3. Verified Historical and Technical Context
- **Asteroid Belt Pioneer**: The first spacecraft to venture into the Asteroid Belt (2.2 to 3.3 AU); verified that asteroid spatial distribution is sparse and dust hazards were manageable, paving the way for Voyager 1 and 2.
- **First Encounter with Jupiter**: First human artifact to fly past Jupiter, transmitting over 300 close-up photographs, discovering the Jovian magnetic tail extending past Saturn, and surviving radiation levels 1,000 times higher than lethal human limits.
- **Power System**: Four SNAP-19 Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generators (RTGs) utilizing Plutonium-238 ($^{238}PuO_2$), generating 155 W at launch and decaying over three decades.
- **The Pioneer Plaque**: An engraved 6061-T6 gold-anodized aluminum plate ($152 \times 229 \times 1.27 \text{ mm}$) bolted to the antenna support struts; features the hyperfine transition of neutral hydrogen ($21 \text{ cm}$), 14 pulsar timing vectors identifying Earth's location and launch epoch, human male and female figures, and a diagram of the Solar System.
- **Interstellar Destination**: Coasting at $12.0 \text{ km/s}$ ($2.5 \text{ AU/year}$) toward the red giant star Aldebaran (alpha Tauri, 65 light-years away), arriving in approximately 2,000,000 years.

---

### 4. Environment Description
- **Interstellar Void**: Absolute cosmic vacuum devoid of atmospheric gas or diffuse scattering; pitch-black background populated by pinpoint stars and the luminous arch of the Milky Way.
- **Distant Sun**: Sun rendered as an intensely brilliant, needle-sharp point-light source ($m_v \approx -19$) casting razor-sharp, zero-penumbra shadows.
- **Jupiter Flyby Stage**: Immense banded gas giant sphere with realistic swirling ammonia clouds, atmospheric turbulence, and the Great Red Spot casting warm orange and cream albedo light across the spacecraft.
- **Aldebaran Star Field**: Distant reddish-orange K5III giant star shining prominently in the background starfield of Taurus.

### 5. Artifact Modelling Requirements
- **High-Gain Antenna (HGA)**: 2.74-meter diameter parabolic aluminum reflector dish painted reflective white with a central horn feed support tripod.
- **Equipment Compartment**: Hexagonal magnesium-alloy avionics bus ($36 \text{ cm}$ deep, $76 \text{ cm}$ flat-to-flat).
- **Deployable RTG Booms**: Two 3-meter triangular tubular truss booms holding pairs of SNAP-19 RTGs with circular cooling fins.
- **Magnetometer Boom**: Long 6.6-meter deployable fiberglass boom holding the vector helium magnetometer.
- **Pioneer Plaque**: Gold-anodized aluminum plate mounted to the antenna dish support struts, with high-resolution vector normal maps replicating the exact 1972 line engraving.
- **Attitude Control**: Cold nitrogen gas thruster clusters mounted on the perimeter of the parabolic dish.
