## 2. Scientific & Engineering Principles

1. **Ultra-Low Density Rotor Aerodynamics**:
   - Atmospheric density at Martian datum is $\rho \approx 0.015 - 0.020 \text{ kg/m}^3$ (~1.2% of Earth's sea-level density $\rho_0 = 1.225 \text{ kg/m}^3$).
   - Equivalent to terrestrial aerodynamic conditions at an altitude of over 30,000 meters (100,000 feet).
   - High chord-to-length ratio, specialized low-Reynolds-number cambered airfoils, and counter-rotating coaxial carbon-fiber composite rotor blades spinning at 2,400 to 2,700 RPM to generate sufficient lift ($L = \frac{1}{2} \rho v^2 S C_L$) for the 1.8 kg (4.0 lb) craft.
2. **Coaxial Counter-Rotating Torque Cancellation**:
   - Two upper and lower 1.2-meter rotor disks rotating in opposite directions eliminate net reactionary torque, removing the necessity of an anti-torque tail rotor.
   - Dual collective and cyclic swashplates powered by micro-servos for pitch, roll, and yaw authority.
3. **Historical Linkage to Aeronautical Heritage**:
   - A $13 \times 13 \text{ mm}$ swatch of unbleached muslin fabric ("Pride of the West") from the lower-left wing of the Wright brothers' 1903 *Wright Flyer* taped with Kapton film underneath the solar panel array.
4. **Visual-Inertial Odometry (VIO) in Low-Texture Terrain**:
   - Navigation relied on a downward-looking monochrome sensor capturing 30 frames/sec to track visual surface features against IMU dead-reckoning.
   - Flight 72 hazard: Traversing the featureless, monotonous sand ripples of Neretva Vallis / Valinor Hills led to navigational optical flow degradation, causing an unexpected descent velocity anomaly and severe rotor tip ground collision.
5. **Post-Mission Operational Transition**:
   - Though grounded by the loss of aerodynamic blade balance (tip delamination on all four blades), Ingenuity's avionics, solar array, and environmental sensors remain active as a long-term automated weather station and testbed.
